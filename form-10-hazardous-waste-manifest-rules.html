Form 10 Hazardous Waste Manifest: The Complete 7-Copy Rules, Tracking Procedure & Audit Guide for Indian Factories

In India, every liter of hazardous waste or used lubricating oil leaving an industrial factory gate must be accounted for under the statutory Manifest System (Form 10) mandated by Rule 19 of the Hazardous and Other Wastes (Management and Transboundary Movement) Rules, 2016. Failing to execute or archive this document exposes factory owners, environmental officers, and plant directors to strict legal prosecution and severe environmental compensation penalties.

The "Cradle-to-Grave" Legal Principle

Under Indian environmental jurisprudence, the waste generator remains strictly liable for their hazardous waste until it is physically delivered to, accepted by, and authenticated by a State Pollution Control Board (SPCB/TNPCB) authorized recycler. Form 10 is your primary legal defense verifying that liability has been officially transferred to an authorized actual-user facility.

1. What is the Form 10 Manifest?

A hazardous waste manifest is a formal multi-copy consignment receipt that travels with hazardous waste shipments from the point of generation (sender) through transport to the final destination (authorized TSDF or recycler).

Unlike commercial bills of lading or delivery challans, Form 10 is a statutory environmental passport. It records the chemical classification, physical state, container count, emergency instructions, vehicle GPS identifiers, and the authorized signatories across all three handling parties: the Generator, the Transporter, and the Recycler.

2. The 7-Copy Color-Coded Distribution Flow

Under Rule 19, the sender (factory) must prepare seven copies of Form 10 with designated color codes. All copies must be signed by the sender and the driver before the transport tanker leaves the factory gate:

Copy & Color Primary Holder Statutory Action & Logistics Flow
Copy 1 (White) Sender's SPCB / TNPCB Forwarded directly by the waste generator to their local District Environmental Engineer (DEE / SPCB) immediately after dispatching the consignment.
Copy 2 (Yellow) Sender (Factory Generator) Retained immediately on-site by the factory after getting the transporter's signed receipt. Kept in the plant's active compliance binder.
Copy 3 (Pink) Receiver (Recycler / TSDF) Retained permanently by the authorized recycling plant (e.g., Green Way Industries) upon unloading, testing, and accepting the waste consignment.
Copy 4 (Orange) Transporter Handed to the vacuum tanker driver by the recycler upon safe delivery as proof of completed logistics.
Copy 5 (Green) Receiver's SPCB / TNPCB Submitted by the recycler to their State Pollution Control Board to certify receipt and processing at their plant.
Copy 6 (Blue)
Crucial Audit Copy		 Sender (Factory Generator) Returned by mail/courier to the sender by the recycler after signing and stamping acceptance. Closes the statutory tracking loop.
Copy 7 (Grey) Destination SPCB Forwarded by the receiver in cases where hazardous waste crosses state borders (inter-state movements requiring prior NOC).

3. The 30-Day "Blue Copy" Trap: Why Audits Fail

The most common cause of non-compliance during TNPCB audits in Tamil Nadu industrial corridors (such as Sriperumbudur, Oragadam, Ambattur, and Ranipet) is the absence of matching Blue Copies.

Statutory 30-Day Escalation Mandate (Rule 19(2))

If the waste generator does not receive the signed Copy 6 (Blue) back from the recycling facility within 30 days of shipment, the generator is legally required to immediately submit a written report to the State Pollution Control Board. If an inspection discovers a Yellow Copy without its matching Blue Copy, the board presumes the waste was dumped illegally.

4. Mandatory Fields Checklist for EHS & Plant Managers

Before releasing a vacuum tanker or truck from your loading dock, ensure your Form 10 contains zero clerical errors across these 6 critical parameters:

1. SPCB Authorization Numbers

Verify that your factory's current Hazardous Waste Authorization number and Green Way Industries' valid TNPCB Authorization number are clearly typed.

2. Exact Waste Schedule Codes

Used lubricating oil must be classified as Schedule I, Category 5.1, while oil emulsions and spent coolant are categorized under Category 5.2.

3. Quantity & Physical State

Specify the exact measured volume in Metric Tonnes (MT) or Kiloliters (KL). State physical form clearly: Liquid / Sludge / Oily Emulsion.

4. Transporter & Tanker Reg.

Record the exact vehicle registration number, driver's name, and commercial driving license number of the specialized vacuum tanker.

5. Emergency Protocol Info

Attach the statutory Transport Emergency (TREM) Card (Form 9) detailing handling precautions, personal protective gear, and spill containment steps.

6. Certified Signatures

Ensure authorized plant EHS representatives sign all 7 copies prior to tanker departure. Transporter counter-signs on dock.

5. Connecting Form 10 to Your Form 3 Ledger & Annual Form 4 Returns

A Form 10 manifest does not exist in isolation. It feeds directly into your statutory plant ledger:

  • Form 3 Day-to-Day Register: Every Form 10 manifest number, date of dispatch, waste category, and net metric weight must be entered into your on-site Form 3 register within 24 hours of dispatch.
  • Form 4 Annual Return: On or before June 30th of every financial year, your factory must compile the cumulative quantities from your Form 10 manifests into the annual return submitted to TNPCB / SPCB. Discrepancies between total manifest volume and annual return volume prompt automatic audit notices.
  • 5-Year Archive Rule: Keep all physical manifests organized chronologically in a dedicated fireproof compliance locker for a minimum of 5 years.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Q1: What is Form 10 in hazardous waste management?

Form 10 is a legally binding 7-copy tracking document mandated under Rule 19 of the Hazardous and Other Wastes Rules, 2016. It records the complete cradle-to-grave transfer of hazardous waste from the generator to the authorized transporter and actual-user recycler.

Q2: What are the 7 colors of the Form 10 Manifest and who keeps them?

The 7 copies are: Copy 1 (White - Sender's SPCB); Copy 2 (Yellow - Sender); Copy 3 (Pink - Recycler); Copy 4 (Orange - Transporter); Copy 5 (Green - Recycler's SPCB); Copy 6 (Blue - Sender return); and Copy 7 (Grey - Inter-state SPCB).

Q3: What happens if a factory does not receive the Blue Copy within 30 days?

Under Rule 19(2), if the sender does not receive signed Copy 6 (Blue) within 30 days of dispatch, the factory is legally mandated to report the non-receipt to the State Pollution Control Board. Failure to report makes the factory liable for illegal disposal.

Q4: How long must factories preserve Form 10 manifests for environmental audits?

Factories must maintain physical and digital copies of all Form 10 manifests (especially matching Yellow and Blue copies), alongside Form 3 registers, for a minimum period of 3 to 5 years.

Q5: Can waste oil be transported without a Form 10 manifest?

No. Transporting waste oil or chemical waste without a Form 10 manifest is an illegal offense under the Environment (Protection) Act, resulting in vehicle impoundment, heavy compensation fines, and factory closure orders.
